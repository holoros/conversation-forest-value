# Follow the wood: maps for the Conversation sequel

Two maps for "Cutting a tree can help save a forest, but only if the wood has somewhere to go" (The Conversation, draft, November 2026).

* `2026-11-06_follow-the-wood-maps_DRAFT.R` builds both maps in R with ggplot2, maps, mapdata and ggrepel.
* `2026-11-06_follow-the-wood-map_DRAFT.png`: schematic routes (Saint John to Macon, Brazil to U.S. buyers) and Maine's 2023 outbound and inbound wood.
* `2026-11-06_maine-mill-towns-map_DRAFT.png`: Jay, Madison, Lincoln, Saco and Orono.

Routes are schematic and show origin and destination, not shipping paths. Figures cited on the maps come from J.D. Irving, Rabobank, the Maine Forest Service 2023 Wood Processor Report (via maineforestdashboard.com), Maine TREE Foundation, Boston Globe, Mainebiz, UMaine News, Maine Public and the UMaine Process Development Center.

Graphic: Center for Research on Sustainable Forests, University of Maine.

Note (November 10, 2026): the Millinocket and Ashland (WholeTrees) markers and labels on the mill towns map were added to the PNG by hand after the R run, and the Millinocket label was shifted left to clear the Ashland marker; the R script does not yet draw them.

Note (November 10, 2026): the follow the wood map was rerun in R with label collisions cleared and the Brazil route ending on the U.S. coast. `2024_maine-wood-exports-world-map_DATA.png` is the Maine Forest Dashboard's world export map (U.S. Census Bureau, USA Trade Online, 2024), copied here for use in the article with white side margins added to fit the figure slot.

Note (November 10, 2026): `2026-11-10_follow-the-wood-leakage-map_DRAFT.png` and its R script put the article's routes over a carbon leakage layer. The layer (`2025_daigneault-erl-fig2a-leakage-layer_DATA.png`, with its legend bar) is cropped from figure 2a of Daigneault, Sohngen, Belair and Ellis (2025), Environmental Research Letters 20:114022, https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae0ce2, used under CC BY 4.0, and georeferenced by fitting coastline extremes. It shows mean carbon leakage for extended rotations at a 10% implementation rate. It is an image of the published figure, not the underlying data.

Note (November 10, 2026): files ending in `-nofooter` are the same figures with the source and credit lines cropped off the bottom for the article, where sources and credits are carried in the captions instead.

Note (November 10, 2026): `2026-11-10_maine-mill-towns-quebec-mills-map_DRAFT.png` replaces the mill towns map in the article. It shows seven Maine towns that lost a paper mill since 2008 (Bangor Daily News, September 26, 2019; Boston Globe for Jay; Old Town's 2023 closure), new uses in Madison, Ashland and Saco, and the Quebec wood mills within 50 km of the Maine border. The Quebec points come from the Gouvernement du Quebec list of permitted primary wood processing mills (CC BY 4.0, file updated June 25, 2026); the 46 mills within 50 km are in `2026-06-25_quebec-mills-within-50km-of-maine_DATA.csv`. Distance is measured to the Maine outline in the R maps package, so it is approximate. New Brunswick mills are not mapped. The image carries no footer; sources are in the article caption.

Note (November 10, 2026, later): the mill map now has a forest layer and the Nackawic, New Brunswick, pulp mill, which its owner idled in October 2026 (CTV News, September 17, 2026). The forest layer (`2020_cec-land-cover-forest-layer_DATA.png`) holds the forest classes of the 2020 Land Cover of North America at 30 meters (Commission for Environmental Cooperation 2023, North American Land Change Monitoring System), exported from the CEC map service for the map frame (longitude -73.6 to -65.4, latitude 42.95 to 47.95). The R script reads that file as `forest_layer.png` and the Quebec list as `qc_usines.csv`.

Note (November 10, 2026, evening): the Saint John pulp mill marker and label and the "Who owns the forest" box were added to the mill map PNG by hand because the R host was offline; the R script does not yet draw them. Ownership shares: Maine 92% private (Maine Forest Service, State of the Forest 2021-2025 review draft, 2023 FIA data); Quebec 92% public province-wide (Quebec forest inventory, as cited by the Montreal Economic Institute, October 29, 2020); New Brunswick 51% Crown land (Government of New Brunswick, Our Forests Are for Everyone).

Note (November 10, 2026, night): the mill map is now built by `2026-11-10_maine-mill-towns-quebec-mills-map_DRAFT.py` (Python and matplotlib, because the R host was offline), which supersedes the R script and the hand edits. Outlines come from the npm packages us-atlas (states-10m) and world-atlas (countries-10m). New in this version: red X for closed or idled pulp and paper mills; orange squares for pulp mills still operating in Rumford, Skowhegan, Baileyville and Saint John, with recent investments (Portland Press Herald, August 27, 2026, for Rumford and Skowhegan; Irving Pulp & Paper's 2024 announcement for Saint John; Bangor Daily News, December 17, 2025, for Baileyville); Quebec dots sized by the upper bound of each mill's permitted volume class; and a full legend. Run it as `python3 <script> <node_modules dir> <out.png>`.

Note (November 10, 2026, late): the mill map adds Sappi's Westbrook mill as a paper mill still operating (hollow orange square). Sappi shut Westbrook's Paper Machine No. 9 and parts of its energy complex in 2020 and kept making release papers there (Sappi news release, July 9, 2020). Forest is now a legend entry. Note (November 10, 2026, final pass): the mill map now shows all five Maine mills that reporting counts as still operating: pulp mills in Rumford, Skowhegan and Baileyville, and paper mills in Westbrook and Madawaska. Madawaska's status is from the Bangor Daily News (April 14, 2026); Westbrook's from a Sappi company profile (170 employees); Baileyville's $150 million tissue expansion from the Bangor Daily News (August 31, 2016). The Edmundston, New Brunswick, pulp mill is not mapped.

## 2026-11-11 mill map: Maine and New Brunswick sawmills, pulp and paper merged

- Added 32 sawmills as unsized purple dots from `2026-11-11_maine-new-brunswick-sawmills_DATA.csv`: 16 Maine softwood mills from the NELMA member mills directory (read November 11, 2026) and the 16 New Brunswick sawmills inside the map frame from the Logging & Sawmilling Journal 2023 mill directory map (3 more fall outside the frame). Placement is at town level. Hardwood and non-member Maine mills are not included.
- Pulp and paper mills still operating now share one solid orange square.
- Removed the "Other New Brunswick mills are not mapped" note.
- Later on November 11, 2026: added the Golden Road and Stud Mill Road from `2026-11-11_golden-and-stud-mill-roads_DATA.csv` (U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb named local road segments, simplified, joined across unnamed gaps with straight lines), and Madison now carries a green triangle under its red X (closed mill with a new use).
- Also November 11, 2026: all sawmills and other wood mills now share one blue; schematic arrows show Maine logs crossing to Quebec and lumber returning to U.S. buyers (Maine TREE Foundation; Bangor Daily News, December 17, 2025); Millinocket carries a green triangle under its red X for the planned biofuel plant at the mill site (Maine Public, January 31, 2025).
- Also November 11, 2026: Maine and New Brunswick sawmills sized in three classes (under 50, 50 to 149, 150 or more million board feet of lumber a year) from the public figures recorded per mill in the sawmill data file; 16 of 32 have a figure, 6 of those from a combined company figure divided evenly, and the other 16 are drawn as open circles. Classes are drawn at 25, 100, and 175 million board feet and converted at about 3.5 cubic meters of logs per thousand board feet (chained from two factors in FAO, ITTO and UNECE 2020, Forest product conversion factors) to sit on the Quebec permit volume scale. The per-mill quotes came through a summarizing fetch and need a spot check before publication.
- Final pass, November 11, 2026: sizes are approximate, for illustration. Six J.D. Irving mills in New Brunswick are sized from the company total (1.3 billion board feet) less its four mills with their own figures, divided evenly; nine mills with no public figure are drawn at the smallest size; Interfor Miramichi was dropped (the company's 2025 Annual Information Form lists it as a woodlands division). Arrow width follows the 2023 totals of raw wood leaving Maine unprocessed (about 2.0 million green tons) and entering (2.7 million), all neighbors combined; the lumber arrow was removed because no flow figure was found.
- Trade arrows, November 11, 2026: arrows now show 2023 Maine and Canada trade from Statistics Canada's Canadian International Merchandise Trade database (`2023_maine-canada-wood-trade-statcan_DATA.csv`; REST calls of the form https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/cimt/rest/getReport/(1)/9/19/{44|47|48}/0/150000/{0 exports|1 imports}/1/2023-01-01/2023-12-01, where 19 is Maine). Maine sent Canada 1.59 million cubic meters of logs and 0.43 million metric tons of chips and fuelwood, and received 0.78 million cubic meters of lumber, 0.38 million tons of pulp, and 0.06 million tons of paper. Arrow widths use approximate weights (about 1 ton per cubic meter of green logs and 0.5 per cubic meter of lumber). A note gives Canada's June 3, 2026 federal forest sector funding (Natural Resources Canada).
